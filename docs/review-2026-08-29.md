# Review 2026-08-29 — the corridor dash

## The report

User, run `20260829T170745` (debug on, three frames): *"empezó a hacer algo muy
tonto: primero hizo un ataque a una pirámide y después hizo un dash donde no
había nada."*

## What the log says

| n | reason | action | result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | explore right | attack (3,2) | garra charged 59 → 58, `broken: false`, "no visual effect - retrying before disabling" |
| 1 | corridor dash | dash | `dash_path: {pyramids: 1, visible_items: 0}`, dash 60 → 59, `energy_delta: 0` |
| 2 | explore right | move (4,1) | board advanced 3 columns, wall gone |

The dash worked — reconstructing the board across n=1 → n=2 only closes at a
3-column advance, so it did break the wall (`scroll.measured: 1` is the sensor
undercounting, not the world). What it did *not* do is justify its price: it
crossed **one** pyramid, and the garra already paid for that same pyramid one
frame earlier was thrown away.

## Root cause

`corridor_dash_due` is priced on a specific story: the first garra **broke**
something, the belt brought a second pyramid into the same row, and a dash now
breaks up to three for the price of the second garra. It tests that story with
`last_attack = (row, frame)` — a row and a frame number, nothing about whether
the attack landed.

A garra whose tap showed no visual effect breaks nothing. The strategy simply
re-proposes the same attack on the same standing pyramid; the row still holds
exactly one. To `corridor_dash_due` that is indistinguishable from the second
garra it was built for.

## The corpus: the rule had never once done what it says

Every corridor dash ever recorded, across every run in `runs/`:

| run | n | previous garra | pyramids crossed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20260824T051703 | 48 | `broken=False`, retrying | 1 |
| 20260827T215725 | 292 | `broken=False`, retrying | 1 |
| 20260829T170745 | 1 | `broken=False`, retrying | 1 |
| 20260828T233043 | 85 | broke (1,1) | 1 |

Four firings. **Three fired on a garra that had broken nothing.** And all four
crossed exactly one pyramid — never the "up to three" the trade is priced on.

## Fix

`corridor_dash_due` takes `attack_unresolved` and refuses while the previous
garra is still marked no-effect (`attack_noeffect_streak > 0`). Two tests pin
both sides: n=0-1 of today's run must not dash, n=83-85 of 20260828T233043 —
where the garra really did break (1,1) and (1,2) scrolled in behind it — must
still dash.

## Still open, deliberately not fixed here

Even the surviving case crossed one pyramid. At the measured economy
(paso 0.455 · garra 0.265 · **dash 0.048** energy per shard) a dash that breaks
one pyramid and advances three cells is worse than a garra plus three steps.
The honest conclusion may be that the rule should require **two** pyramids in
the dash path, not a sixth-column preview — which would retire it entirely, as
`pair_dash_pays` already covers two. That is a bigger change than the reported
bug, so it is written down here rather than made silently.

## Not diagnosed: the looping wall

The user also paused an earlier, unlogged session that *"quedó dando vueltas"*
against a pyramid barrier. It is not in any recorded run: the longest stretch
without a scroll in today's three runs is 14 frames (`20260829T152904`
n=141-156), and that run ends normally at n=599 on budget, at the same (3,1)
against the same forming wall the 17:07 run opens on. Nothing to root-cause
without the frames — it needs a debug run.

# Second pass — the user was right, the log was wrong

## "La garra sí rompió el bloque, yo lo vi"

Correct, and the first pass above accepted the log's word over the user's.
Reading the two frames directly settles it:

| cell | n=0 (before the tap) | n=1 (after) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| **(3,2) — attacked** | **0.93** | **0.63** |
| (0,1) | 0.99 | 0.99 |
| (1,2) | 0.99 | 0.99 |
| (2,2) | 0.98 | 0.95 |
| (4,1) | 0.98 | 0.99 |
| (4,3) | 0.98 | 0.99 |

The attacked cell moved by 0.30; the five pyramids sharing its screen moved
by at most 0.03. It was dissolving. `attack_result` asked only "is this still
above 0.45?", so it said "no visual effect".

A pyramid does not vanish between two frames. Across the whole corpus — 273
attacks, against as control the 1,017 untouched pyramids standing in the *same*
frame pairs (an attack does not scroll, so the two frames align cell for cell):

- attacked cells drop more than 0.25 in **96.0%** of taps;
- untouched pyramids drop more than 0.25 in **5.4%**.

Reading the attacked cell against itself flips **32 of 273** taps to broken and
**zero** the other way. One garra in nine was being reported dead when it had
worked.

## That is also the loop

The price was never the 200 shards of the retry. Two no-effect readings in a
row disable garras (`should_disable_attacks`), and on the barrier board the run
opens with:

```
col      0     1     2     3     4   | 5
row 0    .     P     .     .     .   |  .
row 1    .     .     P     .     .   |  .
row 2    .     .     P     .     .   |  .
row 3    .    bot    P     .     .   |  P
row 4    .     P     .     P     .   |  .
```

Column 2 is walled on rows 1-3, and the two ways around it — rows 0 and 4 — are
corked at column 1. Without garras there is **no move that reaches column 2 at
all**, so every step is one the bot will have to take back. That is the wide
loop the user watched. Not indecision: a bot whose only tool had been switched
off by a false reading. `tests/test_pyramid_barrier.py` pins the board from the
frames themselves.

Every one of the three "no visual effect" events in run `20260829T152904`
(n=151, 221, 407) is on the flip list.

## Correction to §11 of review-2026-08-28: the preview is good

Yesterday's "precision 0.509" was measured against the wrong ground truth. The
runs label the sliver for free: the strip **is** column 5, so a one-column
scroll turns it into column 4, a full cell the detector reads well. 4,845
labelled readings over 32 runs, split **by run**, tuned on 21 and reported once
on the 11 held out:

| strip | top | blue-over-green | thr | holdout precision | recall |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| .18 cell | 55% | +10 (until now) | .5 | 0.869 | 0.902 |
| **.25 cell** | **55%** | **+18** | **.3** | **0.941** | **0.916** |

The lever is the blue-over-green margin, not the width: the board's own frame
decor beside the strip is bluish too, and +10 lets it through. Demanding +18
separates the pyramid glass from the decor, which then *allows* a lower coverage
threshold — a pyramid only half in view still fills 0.3 of a strip that no
longer counts the frame.

## Two things the user proposed, measured and not built

- **Put the sliver in memory / vote it across frames.** Worse on every variant
  tried: median-of-3 > .5 gives 0.768 / 0.717, ANY-of-2 gives 0.702 / 0.905 —
  all below the single frame. The reading is not noisy frame to frame; it is
  right or wrong for the whole dwell, so averaging only spreads the wrong ones.
- **Use the prediction to refine full-cell pyramid detection.** There is almost
  nothing to refine: only **13 of 2,260** arrivals (0.6%) land in the ambiguous
  0.45-0.90 band. The full cell is its own answer. The ambiguity worth fixing
  was never at column 4 — it was the attacked cell, and that is the fix above.

# Third pass — the law, checked against the code

User statement of the physics, 2026-08-29:

> En ningún momento va a aparecer algo nuevo en las columnas 0, 1, 2 y 3.
> Todo aparece en la columna 4. La quinta columna te deja previsualizar si
> viene alguna pirámide, pero ningún otro drop. Cuando se destruye una
> pirámide con un ataque debería aparecer, sin moverse el tablero, algún tipo
> de drop; si no, una casilla vacía.

Checked clause by clause against `world_model.py`. The model already encodes
all of it, and none of it is over-built:

| clause | where it lives | status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| new content only through the right edge | `_classify` → `right_edge` when `cell[1] >= BOARD - shift` | already exact |
| an attack changes a cell in place | `_classify` → `reveal`, fed by `attack_result`'s `revealed` | already exact |
| first screenshot is the exception | `_classify` → `initial` on `first_frame` | already exact |
| column 5 announces pyramids and nothing else | `sixth_column_preview` returns five booleans, pyramid only | already exact |
| a suspicion in column 5 confirms in column 4 | `_absorb_preview` → `_predicted[row]`, then `_confirmed` when a pyramid is born `right_edge` in that row | already exact |
| three in a row and no better target → go to that row | `nearest_dash_wall(preview=...)` counts column 5 as a pyramid when the row is lit | already exact |

The one place the code contradicted the law was `attack_result`, which is the
bug fixed in the second pass: it let a *destroyed* pyramid go on being reported
as standing.

## A tightening the law seemed to license, measured, and REFUTED

If nothing can be born in columns 0-3, then a pyramid appearing there is a
misdetection - and `Track.believed` returns True for any pyramid, on sight. On
450 frame pairs where the board provably did not move (every pyramid the
previous frame saw still in the same cell - stronger evidence than
`scroll.measured`, a sensor that undercounts), **56 pyramids were born in
columns 0-3 anyway**, one pair in eight.

So: make an interior-born pyramid earn `CONFIRM_SIGHTINGS` like a card does.
Two of the repo's own tests failed immediately, and both were right:

- `test_confetti_over_a_pyramid_does_not_replace_it` (run 20260822T160202 n=89)
- `test_a_refuted_cell_can_be_seen_again_later` - "the eyes are the authority in
  both directions: a real pyramid that was momentarily invisible must be able
  to come back"

Splitting the 56 by whether the cell had held a pyramid in the previous five
frames says the same thing with numbers: **24 of 56 are recoveries**, 17 of
them of a pyramid invisible for exactly one frame. And the measurement was
mis-aimed anyway - it counted raw per-frame detections, not tracks, so most of
those 24 never become births in the real model at all: `MAX_MISSES = 3` already
carries a pyramid through three unseen frames for exactly this reason.

Reverted. The law does not distinguish "appeared" from "was finally seen", and
the model's existing miss tolerance is the right place for that difference. The
change is not deferred - it is refused, with the evidence above.

# Fourth pass — one cell, one thing

User law: *"No puede haber más de una cosa en cada celda. Las pirámides no pueden
tener items reales encima. Solo pueden aparecer items si se destruyen con un
ataque."* Ran it, and it holds — the code did not.

## Which colour can even be a pyramid

Swept exhaustively over the RGB cube against this module's own glass test
(`b > 70 and r > 45 and b > g + 10`):

| family | colours | of them also pyramid glass |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| green | 371,046 | **0 (0.00%)** |
| orange | 123,950 | 4,921 (3.97%) |
| pink | 192,660 | **187,150 (97.14%)** |

Pink **is** glass. That is the whole reason `is_obstacle` carried an item veto,
and it keeps it. Green and orange are a different colour, so they were never the
pixels that made the pyramid score high — and by the law, a green or orange
reading over a standing pyramid is confetti painted **on** it.

## What the blanket veto cost

Over 1,182 recorded frames, cells the detector handed the planner as pickups:

| pyramid score of a "pickup" cell | before | after |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| above .45 (the obstacle threshold) | 153 (7.21%) | **19 (0.96%)** |
| above .60 | 106 (5.00%) | **5 (0.25%)** |

101 cells read pyramid .63-.99 with no pink at all and were offered as pickups —
one frame in twelve. Every one is a tap onto a pyramid, which the game charges
as a hidden 200-shard garra (run 20260822T160202 n=89). The 19 that remain are
pink, which is the veto doing its job.

Run 20260828T215949 n=77 is the picture of it: a board mid-confetti-burst, plain
glass pyramids outlined as items.

## The other half: the planner never asked

Making `is_obstacle` stricter alone made the replay corpus flag **35
STARVATIONs** — "choose() proposes move to (4,2) that the tap gate refuses". The
cause is older than today's change: `orange_items` and `other_items` are built
straight from the raw colour scores with no obstacle guard, while
`unsafe_move_tap` refuses the tap as a pyramid. `claw_items` has had that guard
since 2026-08-22; those two never did. The planner paced toward a cell the gate
would always say no to. Guarded, and `pickup_type` now returns `None` for an
obstacle so that one definition serves every reader.

## Measured and NOT built: the apex in the denominator

The user's second precision complaint — an item above a pyramid loses precision
because the apex pokes into its cell — is real in mechanism and negligible in
size. The item score is a fraction of the whole cell, so the apex sits in its
denominator; taking pyramid-glass pixels out of the bottom 45% of cells that sit
directly above a pyramid rescues **7 cells in 1,182 frames**, every one a
marginal 0.05 → 0.06. Not worth a new code path.

The wider version — dropping glass pixels from the denominator everywhere —
rescues 255, but only 22% of those have a pyramid below at all, and the big
jumps (0.021 → 0.117) are cells that are *mostly* glass, i.e. pyramids with a
speck of colour on them. Applying it would manufacture items on pyramids: the
exact opposite of the law. Refused.

## Still open: garras occasionally ignored

Not explained yet. The border veto — a claw whose yellow touches the cell edge —
kills only **6 cells in 1,182 frames**, and none of them sits above a pyramid,
so that is not the mechanism. Needs a frame where it happens.

# Fifth pass — la energía que no recogió: una pirámide fantasma inmortal

Run `20260829T201007`, stopped by the user around n=83: *"no recogió una energía,
es como si se hubiera descachado."*

## What happened

| n | player | board says | action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 76 | (0,1) | oranges at (1,2) and (1,4) | move **down** to (1,1) |
| 77 | (1,1) | orange only at (1,4); **(1,2) listed as a pyramid** | move **down** to (2,1) |
| 78 | (2,1) | orange at (1,2) again | move right ×2 |
| 80 | (2,1) | oranges at (1,0) and (1,2) | move **up** to (1,1) |
| 81 | (1,1) | orange at (1,2) | takes it |

At n=77 the bot stood at (1,1) with a real orange one step to its right and
walked away from it. Four paws to come back for it.

Re-scoring every frame in the window, n=77 is **the only one where the logged
board and the pixels disagree**: the frame has no pyramid at (1,2) (glass 0.066
at n=76, 0.066 at n=78) but the runner's board has one. Replaying that exact
frame through the harness, `choose()` answers `move right to (1,2)` — *"adjacent
item=(1,2)"*. The strategy was never the problem.

## The ghost

The pyramid at **(1,4)** left the screen at n=73 and an orange took the cell.
Two lines conspired to make its track immortal:

- `observe`'s decay pass skips any cell in `seen_cells`, and an orange sighting
  puts the cell there — so the dead pyramid was never aged.
- `_update`'s covered-pyramid branch (*"a card painted over a pyramid does not
  turn it into a pickup"*) reset `misses = 0` on every one of those sightings,
  undoing even the misses it might have accrued.

So the track rode the belt left with the world — (1,4) → (1,3) → (1,2) — and at
n=77 `covered_pyramids` stamped `pyramid = .9` on it and, per
`merge_phantom_obstacles`, zeroed every item channel. The real orange was erased
from the board the planner saw.

## The rule that fixes it

The cover premise is right; it just has no expiry. Confetti does not last:
measured over 709 quiet frame pairs, **87.8%** of interior pickup sightings are
gone by the next frame and **95.4%** within two. An item still standing on the
third frame is an item, and the pyramid that was on that cell is gone.

So a covered pyramid track now ages like anything else and dies at
`MAX_MISSES = 3` — and instead of being deleted, it is **handed to the item that
was there all along**, keeping the sightings it accrued. Deleting it would make
the pickup a newborn: unexplained, three more sightings before it can be acted
on, for something the screen has been showing for three frames. By the law it
cannot have arrived there — nothing is born in the interior.

Both sides are pinned: one frame of confetti still leaves the pyramid standing
(run 20260822T160202 n=89), a cover that never lifts does not.

# Sixth pass — la luz de "puedes pisar aquí" contaba como pirámide

Run `20260829T204601`, stopped by the user around n=27: *"dios mío estamos
retrocediendo. Decidió alejarse de una energía (que justamente tiene una punta
de pirámide desde abajo) y decidió hacer un paso atrás tonto y alejarse."*

The user's parenthesis is the whole diagnosis.

## What happened

| n | player | (3,2) on the board | action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 20 | (2,1) | orange | move down to (3,1) — heading for it |
| **21** | **(3,1)** | **listed as a pyramid** | **move LEFT to (3,0)** |
| 22 | (3,0) | orange again, but `planner_goals` = [(0,4)] only | up ×2 |
| 24 | (1,0) | orange again, `planner_goals` = [(0,4)] | up to (0,0) |
| 25 | (0,0) | orange, back in the goals | right ×2 |

Five paws to walk away from an orange it was standing next to, and back.

## The cause, cell (3,2) frame by frame

| n | glass | orange | highlight | verdict |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 20 | 0.071 | 0.250 | 0.055 | orange |
| **21** | **0.468** | 0.129 | **0.767** | **pyramid** |
| 22 | 0.067 | 0.130 | 0.077 | orange |
| 24 | 0.068 | 0.132 | 0.083 | orange |
| 25 | 0.068 | 0.130 | 0.091 | orange |

The glass spikes on exactly the frame where the bot is standing beside the cell.
The pyramid mask is `b > 70 and r > 45 and b > g + 10`; the walkable highlight is
`b > 120 and g > 90 and b > r + 25` — **bright blue, and it satisfies the pyramid
test.** The game lighting a cell as a legal move was being counted as evidence
that it is a pyramid.

Run `20260829T201007` n=77 is the same shape: 0.066, then **0.450**, then 0.066,
on the frame the bot stepped next to it.

And the pyramid-only item veto shipped earlier today is what turned that
inflation from harmless into board-erasing: with the veto gone for orange, the
spike crossed the threshold and deleted a real pickup. **My regression.**

## The fix

Take the highlight pixels out of the pyramid mask. A cell the game will let you
step into cannot be a pyramid — that is the game's own statement, and it outranks
our colour arithmetic.

Over 1,139 recorded frames:

- of the **3,850** cells reading raw glass above .90 — pyramids beyond argument —
  **all** stay above .40 once the light is removed (worst .438, p01 .468);
- **every one** of the 50 cells the highlight had pushed over the line from the
  .30-.60 band falls back under it.

`PYRAMID_THRESHOLD` moves .45 → .40 with the scale: a solid pyramid now reads
about .54, not .95, because the highlight was part of what it was reading.

## A finding from this morning, re-measured and mostly dissolved

`BREAK_DROP` was introduced today because the absolute rule reported 32 of 273
landed garras as "no visual effect". Re-measured over the same 382 frame pairs
with the light out of the mask: the absolute rule now agrees on **all 292**, and
the drop rule rescues **zero**. Nearly every false "no visual effect" was the bot
standing beside the cell it had just hit, with the game's own highlight holding
the score above the line. The rule stays as a backstop (retuned to .20 for the
new scale) because it costs nothing, but it is no longer load-bearing.

The barrier fixture shows it: (3,2) used to read .93 → .63 across the garra;
it now reads **.55 → .03**.

# Séptimo paso — el confetti, medido contra el contador del juego

User, after the run stopped at n=27: *"me parece increible que aun no logremos
recordar items reales y no dejarnos confundir por una animacion corta de
confetti."* Fair. Here is the measurement that had never been made.

## The ground truth nobody was using

Every frame logs the **energy counter**. An orange is +125. So every recorded
step onto a cell the board showed as an orange grades itself: it either paid or
it did not. **761 such steps across 45 runs**, no opinion involved.

**33.1% of them collected nothing.** One step in three onto a "visible orange"
was walking onto an animation.

## Confetti announces itself by crowding the board

Grouping those 761 steps by how many items the frame showed:

| items on screen | 1 | 2 | 3 | **4** | 5 | 6 | 7+ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| really there | **99.2%** | **93.3%** | 73.3% | **26.2%** | 14.7% | 23.1% | 6.8% |

A **cliff between three and four**, not a slope. The board simply does not hold
four pickups at once very often; when it looks like it does, a collection just
happened and the cards are still in the air.

## The rule

`BURST_ITEMS = 4`: a frame showing four or more pickups **is not evidence about
pickups**. It is unobservable, exactly the way the partner's own body already
makes its 3x3 unobservable - tracks under it neither age nor gain confidence,
and nothing new is born from it. Its sightings are handed to the planner as
suspects so the strategy, which builds goals from the raw colour scores, does
not walk to them anyway.

What it deliberately does **not** do is forget. An item already believed stays
believed straight through the burst. It is a blindfold, never an eraser - that
is the half the user asked for.

## And it re-prices the rule that was doing this job badly

With a causal detector in place, the statistical one could finally be measured.
Splitting the 761 steps **by run**, 15 runs held out:

| rule | real taken | real deferred | confetti refused | confetti taken |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| three sightings, no burst (today) | 74 | 34 | 54 | 8 |
| burst + three sightings | 64 | 44 | 59 | 3 |
| **burst + two sightings** | **84** | **24** | **57** | **5** |
| burst + one sighting | 105 | 3 | 45 | 17 |
| nothing at all | 108 | 0 | 0 | 62 |

`CONFIRM_SIGHTINGS` 3 -> **2**. Burst+two beats today on **both** columns: ten
more real oranges *and* three fewer confetti steps. Waiting three frames was
never confetti-specific - it delayed every real pickup equally.

Burst+one scores higher still on that arithmetic (+31 real for +9 confetti), and
it is **not taken**: nine of this repo's own tests refuse it and the replay
corpus flags real STARVATION violations. Recorded here so the next person does
not have to re-derive why.

Over the whole corpus the shipped pair moves steps onto confetti from
**252 of 761 (33.1%) to 24 (3.2%)**, deferring 118 real oranges by a frame.

## Three tests re-adjudicated, not adjusted

`test_mid_board_appearance_without_a_scroll_is_confetti`,
`test_confetti_never_becomes_memory_by_being_covered` and
`test_a_real_item_is_believed_two_frames_later` each hard-coded "three
sightings" as a frame count. They now state the same principle against
`wm.CONFIRM_SIGHTINGS`, so the principle is pinned and the number stays a
measured knob.

## Regression check, both directions

The curated replay corpus (`tests/test_replay.py` — the 2026-08-22/23 field-bug
runs that are the actual gate) stays at **0 violations**. Across all 45 recorded
runs, the number showing any violation is **19 before these changes and 19
after**: those are recordings taken before their own bugs were fixed, and the
count did not move.
